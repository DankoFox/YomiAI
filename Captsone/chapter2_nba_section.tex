\subsection{Evolution of Next Best Action Systems and the Research Gap}
\label{subsec:nba-evolution}

Sequential recommendation, also called session-based recommendation or next best action (NBA) prediction, has gone through three broad model families over the past fifteen years.

The earliest was Markov-based. \cite{rendle2010fpmc} combined matrix factorization with first-order Markov transitions, which made per-user sequential inference tractable even on sparse data. The core assumption from the start: the most recent interaction is the strongest signal for the next one.

Recurrent networks followed. \cite{hidasi2016gru4rec} showed that Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) outperform Markov models by reading the full sequence through a learned hidden state rather than just the last step. Then \cite{kang2018self} replaced the recurrence with self-attention. SASRec applies a causally masked Transformer to weight past items selectively and outperforms GRU-based approaches on both sparse and dense datasets.

All three share the same limitation: items are ID embeddings. Two books with different ISBNs are orthogonal vectors regardless of how similar they are in content. Models trained on a fixed vocabulary struggle with new or rarely purchased items, and their weights are frozen at serving time, so they cannot adjust as a user's intent shifts mid-session. This is the gap the present system targets.

\cite{wei2023mmssl} showed that aligning visual and textual item signals via contrastive learning produces recommendation signal orthogonal to co-occurrence patterns, which improves cold-start accuracy substantially. \cite{zhou2023bm3} showed that simple dropout-based cross-modal augmentation reaches competitive accuracy at 2--9$\times$ lower training cost than graph-based multimodal methods. \cite{guo2024lgmrec} added local and global graph structure on top of multimodal features and found that visual content and interaction structure contribute independently rather than substituting for each other.

Pipeline~A of the present system uses Cleora co-purchase graph embeddings re-ranked by BGE-M3, capturing the co-occurrence structure that goes back to \cite{rendle2010fpmc}. Pipeline~B extends the decoupled attention architecture from \cite{xie2022difsasrec} with per-user online fine-tuning via \texttt{AgentPool}, allowing visual and categorical features to adjust recommendations within a live session without waiting for offline retraining.
