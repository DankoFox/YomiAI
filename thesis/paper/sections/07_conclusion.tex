\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a dual-mode multimodal NBA recommendation system for book
discovery that combines text, visual, and behavioral signals across active
search and passive recommendation.
The system integrates multimodal encoders (BGE-M3, CLIP) with co-purchase
graph embeddings and DIF-SASRec sequential personalization, evaluated
end-to-end against multiple baselines.

By conditioning DIF-SASRec on dense BGE-M3 content embeddings rather than
item IDs, the system generalizes to the full 3.08M-item catalog without
retraining.
Pipeline~A (behavioral graph embedding + BGE-M3 ranking) provides
content-aware retrieval, while DIF-SASRec delivers sequential
personalization via online adaptation.
Complementarity shows Pipeline~A recovers 88.3\% of DIF-SASRec
misses (Table~\ref{tab:complementarity}), and the union achieves HR@10 of 0.9736
(+124\% over the content-only baseline), with warm-cache P50 of 34\,ms
and safe concurrent inference via the model pool.

Future work includes dynamic graph embedding retraining from interaction
logs, multilingual keyword search beyond the current inverted index,
cross-domain transfer of pretrained DIF-SASRec weights, and extension of
the NBA framing beyond book discovery.
The system shows that heterogeneous retrieval signals can be combined in a
single user-state model that updates with each interaction, without
requiring per-domain retraining.
